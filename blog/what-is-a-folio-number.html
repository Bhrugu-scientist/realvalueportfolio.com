What Is a Folio Number in Mutual Funds?

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · August 2026 · 5 min read
Short answer: A folio number is the unique account ID an AMC gives you the first time you invest with them. Think of it as a bank account number, but for mutual funds — every scheme you buy from that same AMC can sit under the same folio, or you can open a new one each time.

If you've ever looked at a mutual fund statement and wondered what that 10-12 digit number at the top means, it's your folio number. It's small print that matters more than most investors realise — especially when things go wrong and you need to prove what you own.

What a folio number actually does

When you invest in a mutual fund for the first time with a particular AMC — say, through a lump sum or your first SIP instalment — the AMC's registrar (usually CAMS or KFintech) creates a folio in your name. This folio records:

  • Your identity details (PAN, KYC status, bank account, nominee)
  • Every scheme and instalment you've invested in with that AMC
  • Units held, NAV history, and redemption records

If you invest again with the same AMC — even in a completely different scheme — you can either add it to your existing folio or ask for a new one. Most investors don't realise they get a choice; by default many platforms create a fresh folio unless you specify otherwise.

Folio number vs PAN vs CAS: don't confuse these

What it isScope
PANYour tax identity, issued by Income Tax DeptSame across all banks, AMCs, brokers — lifetime
Folio numberAccount ID with one specific AMCOne AMC only; you may have several folios there
CASConsolidated statement showing all folios, all AMCsCombines everything linked to your PAN

So if you invest across five different AMCs, you'll have at least five different folio numbers — possibly more, if you've opened multiple folios within the same AMC over the years. Your PAN is the thread that ties them all together on your CAS.

Why this matters more than it sounds

  • Redemptions and transfers: You need the correct folio number to redeem, switch, or transfer units — especially if you're dealing offline or through a physical form.
  • Nominee and KYC updates: Changes to your bank account, address, or nominee are usually done folio-wise, not account-wise. Update one folio and forget another, and old details linger.
  • Tracking scattered investments: If you've invested via different apps or agents over the years, you may unknowingly have multiple folios with the same AMC. This fragments your holding and makes tax reporting messier at redemption time.
  • Death claims and transmission: When a family member needs to claim units after an investor's death, they need the folio number(s) to even begin the process. A folio no one else knows about is money that can sit unclaimed for years.

    There's no universal rule, but a few points help:

    • Fewer folios per AMC = simpler tracking and statements, especially useful for annual tax filing.
    • Separate folios can help if you're running very distinct goals (e.g., one folio purely for a child's education corpus) and want a clean paper trail.
    • If you've built up multiple folios by accident, most AMCs allow consolidation on request — worth doing once a year as basic portfolio hygiene, alongside checking your NAV and returns.

    How to find your folio number

    1. Check the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) emailed by CAMS or KFintech after any transaction across your PAN.
    2. Look at the welcome letter or email sent when you first invested.
    3. Check any purchase or redemption confirmation SMS/email — the folio number is usually printed at the top.
    4. Ask your mutual fund distributor or RIA — if they helped you invest, they can pull it up in seconds.

    The bottom line

    A folio number isn't glamorous, but it's the paperwork backbone of your mutual fund investing. Know your folio numbers, keep them noted somewhere your family can find (alongside your other investment records), and do an annual check to make sure KYC, nominee, and bank details are current across every one of them. It's five minutes of housekeeping that saves real trouble later.
