Emergency + Goals + Wealth: The 3-Layer Plan

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: Split every rupee you save into three buckets — Emergency (safety, 6 months expenses, liquid), Goals (2–7 year targets, moderate risk), and Wealth (10+ year compounding, mostly equity). Most money mistakes happen when people mix these up — using wealth money for emergencies, or emergency money for wealth.

Ask ten people "where should I invest?" and you'll get ten different fund names. That's the wrong question. The right question is: what is this money for, and when do I need it? Once you answer that, the right product almost picks itself. This 3-layer structure is the simplest way to organise an entire financial life — whether you're saving ₹5,000 a month or ₹5 lakh.

Layer 1: Emergency fund (the shock absorber)

This is money that exists purely so a crisis never becomes a catastrophe — job loss, medical emergency, urgent travel, a broken car. It is not an investment. Its job is to be there, fully intact, the day you need it.

FeatureGuideline
Size6 months of essential expenses (more if income is irregular)
Where it sitsSavings account + liquid/overnight fund mix
Risk toleranceZero — capital safety over returns
Access timeInstant to 1 day

Don't judge this layer by its returns. A 6–7% return that's available instantly, with zero drama, is doing exactly its job. For more on where short-term money belongs, see our FD vs mutual fund comparison.

Layer 2: Goals (the 2–7 year money)

This is money with a name and a date attached — a down payment in 4 years, a child's school admission in 3 years, a car in 2 years, a wedding in 5. These goals are too close to risk heavily in equity, but too far away to leave in a savings account losing value to inflation.

  • 2–3 years out: lean towards debt-oriented or conservative hybrid funds
  • 4–7 years out: a measured equity-debt mix, de-risked as the date approaches
  • Rule of thumb: the closer the goal, the less equity it should carry

The biggest error here isn't picking the wrong fund — it's not labelling the goal at all, so the money drifts and gets spent, or gets invested too aggressively for its timeline and then has to be withdrawn at a loss.

Layer 3: Wealth (the compounding engine)

This is money you genuinely won't touch for 10, 15, 20+ years — retirement, long-term financial independence, legacy. Because the time horizon is so long, short-term market dips simply don't matter; what matters is staying invested and letting compounding do the heavy lifting.

FeatureGuideline
Horizon10+ years
VehiclePredominantly equity mutual funds, via SIP
Biggest riskNot market crashes — exiting too early out of fear
Review frequencyAnnually, not daily

This layer is where real wealth gets built, precisely because it's the one layer you're allowed to ignore the news about. If you want to understand why patience matters more than timing here, our piece on equity vs fixed-return instruments covers the long-term maths.

Putting it together: a sample split

There's no universal percentage — it depends on your age, income stability, and existing savings. But a reasonable starting framework for someone early in their career might look like this:

LayerPriority orderTypical share of monthly savings
EmergencyBuild first, until 6 months coveredHigh initially, then near-zero
GoalsOngoing, goal by goal20–40%
WealthStart early, never stopRemainder, rising over time

Notice the emergency fund isn't a permanent 20% slice — it's a target you fill once, then mostly maintain. After that, almost all new savings can flow into Goals and Wealth.

Why mixing layers causes most financial pain

Nearly every money crisis we see traces back to a layer mix-up: someone put their emergency fund into equity and had to sell during a downturn, or someone poured their retirement money into a 2-year goal and missed a decade of compounding, or someone had no Layer 1 at all and had to break a long-term SIP the moment a medical bill arrived. The structure itself prevents these mistakes — it forces you to ask "which layer is this?" before every rupee moves.

The honest verdict

You don't need a complicated portfolio. You need three clearly labelled buckets, each matched to its own time horizon and risk level, and the discipline to not borrow from one to feed another. Build the emergency fund first, keep goals on track with the right risk level for their timeline, and let the wealth layer run quietly in the background for decades. That's it — that's the whole plan.
