ULIP vs Mutual Fund — The Honest Breakdown (2026)

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: A ULIP mixes insurance and investment into one product with layered charges and a 5-year lock-in. Buying term insurance separately and investing in mutual funds almost always gives you more cover, more flexibility, and better net returns for the same money.

ULIPs are sold hard because they pay a strong commission upfront. That doesn't make them wrong for everyone — but it does mean the pitch is rarely neutral. Here's what the numbers actually show.

What a ULIP actually is

A Unit Linked Insurance Plan splits your premium into two buckets: a small life insurance cover, and the rest invested in market-linked funds (equity, debt, or a mix) chosen from the insurer's own list. You get one product doing two jobs — neither of them optimally.

Head to head

Why "2-in-1" usually costs more, not less

The pitch sounds efficient: one product for protection and growth. But insurance is priced for mortality risk, and investing is priced for market performance — mixing them means you pay insurance-style charges on your investment corpus and get an investment-linked, usually inadequate, life cover. Run this test: check how much life cover a ULIP gives you per ₹1,000 of annual premium, then check what a term plan gives you for the same ₹1,000. The gap is usually stark — often 5-10x more cover from term insurance for the same money.

The lock-in cuts both ways

ULIP defenders point out the 5-year lock-in forces discipline — no panic-selling in a market dip. That's fair. But it also means if the fund inside your ULIP genuinely underperforms, or your priorities change, you're stuck. A mutual fund SIP gives you the same discipline through a simple habit and an accountability check-in, without removing your ability to exit if something is clearly wrong.

What to actually check before buying (or continuing) a ULIP

  • Total charges as % of premium in years 1–5 — ask for this in writing, not verbally
  • Sum assured vs what a term plan of the same premium would give you
  • Fund performance of the ULIP's underlying fund vs a comparable mutual fund category average over 5+ years
  • Surrender value if you exit early — often much lower than what you paid in
  • Whether you're already covered adequately elsewhere, making the ULIP's insurance component redundant

When a ULIP might still make sense

If you have already maxed genuine term cover and mutual fund investing, and specifically want the tax-free maturity treatment for a large annual outlay under ₹2.5 lakh, a ULIP can be one small piece of a plan. It's rarely the right first or only product — but for a very specific tax situation, discuss the numbers with an advisor rather than ruling it out on principle.

The honest verdict

Separate the two jobs. Buy adequate term insurance — it's inexpensive and does one job well. Invest the rest through mutual funds where you can see charges, compare performance, and exit if you need to. If you already hold a ULIP, don't panic-surrender — check the surrender charge and remaining lock-in first, then decide with real numbers, not regret.
