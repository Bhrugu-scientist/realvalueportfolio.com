How to Build a 3-Fund Portfolio

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: Pick one large-cap/flexi-cap fund for stability, one mid/small-cap fund for growth, and one debt or hybrid fund for ballast — then split money between them based on your goal horizon and risk tolerance. That's it. You don't need 12 funds to be diversified.

Walk into most Indian investors' portfolios and you'll find 10, 12, sometimes 20 mutual funds — bought over the years from different agents, apps, and "hot tips." Almost none of them know why they own what they own. A 3-fund portfolio fixes this. It's not about owning fewer things for the sake of it — it's about owning the right three things and understanding each one's job.

Why more funds usually means less diversification

This sounds backwards, but it's true. Most large-cap and flexi-cap funds in India hold overlapping stocks — the same handful of large banks, IT majors, and consumer names show up in fund after fund. Owning eight of these funds doesn't diversify you; it just means you're paying eight expense ratios to hold roughly the same portfolio. Real diversification comes from spreading across categories that behave differently, not from piling up funds within the same category.

The three roles in a 3-fund portfolio

FundJobWhat it does for you
Large-cap / Flexi-capCore stabilitySteadier ride through market cycles, lower volatility than mid/small-cap
Mid-cap / Small-capGrowth engineHigher long-term growth potential, but sharper drawdowns — needs a longer horizon
Debt / HybridBallast & rebalancing fuelCushions the portfolio in equity downturns and gives you "dry powder" to rebalance

Each fund has one job. When you understand the job, you stop panicking when one part of the portfolio does something the others don't — that's the whole point of holding it.

A starting allocation framework

  • Long horizon (10+ years), high risk tolerance: 50% large/flexi-cap, 35% mid/small-cap, 15% debt
  • Medium horizon (5–8 years), moderate risk tolerance: 50% large/flexi-cap, 25% mid/small-cap, 25% debt
  • Shorter horizon (3–5 years) or lower risk tolerance: 45% large/flexi-cap, 15% mid/small-cap, 40% debt

These are starting points, not formulas carved in stone. Your actual split should reflect your age, income stability, other assets (like EPF or real estate), and how you genuinely react — not how you think you'll react — when markets fall 20%.

What to actually check before picking each fund

  • Category consistency: has the fund stayed true to its mandate (large-cap fund actually holding large-caps), or has it drifted?
  • Expense ratio: lower isn't automatically better, but a high expense ratio is a permanent drag you pay every single year
  • Fund manager tenure: a strategy is only as reliable as the person and process executing it — check how long the current manager has run the fund
  • Downside capture: how much did the fund fall relative to its benchmark during past corrections? This tells you more about risk than the return chart does
  • Portfolio overlap: if you already hold a flexi-cap fund, make sure your "different" large-cap fund isn't just holding the same 30 stocks

We don't recommend specific schemes here — that decision depends on your goals, tax situation, and existing holdings. But these are the exact checks to run on any fund before it earns a seat in your three.

Rebalancing: the part everyone skips

A 3-fund portfolio isn't "set once and forget forever." Once a year, check whether your allocation has drifted from target — a strong equity run might push your 50/30/20 split to 60/30/10. Rebalancing back to target isn't about predicting the market; it's a disciplined way to book some gains and top up whatever's lagging, which is usually the opposite of what emotion tells you to do.

When 3 funds isn't quite enough

Some investors reasonably add a fourth sleeve — international equity for currency and geographic diversification, or an ELSS fund purely for the Section 80C tax benefit. That's fine. The principle isn't a magic number; it's that every fund in the portfolio should have a distinct, understood job. The moment you can't explain why a fund is there, it's a candidate for removal, not addition.

The honest verdict

A cluttered portfolio doesn't protect you — it confuses you, and confused investors panic-sell at the worst times. Three well-chosen funds, each with a clear role, properly allocated to your goal and rebalanced once a year, will outperform a messy 15-fund portfolio built from years of ad-hoc decisions — almost every time.
