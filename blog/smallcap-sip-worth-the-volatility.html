Is a Smallcap SIP Worth the Volatility?

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: Yes — if you have a genuine 8-10+ year horizon, keep it to a limited slice of your portfolio, and can promise yourself you won't stop the SIP when it falls 40%. If any of those three conditions isn't true, the volatility isn't worth it for you right now.

Smallcap funds get sold on their best 3-year chart. Nobody shows you the 18 months before that chart, when the same fund was down 45% and every headline said "smallcap bubble bursts." Both charts are real. The question isn't whether smallcaps are good — it's whether you are built to hold through the part nobody screenshots.

What smallcap actually means

Smallcap companies are ranked 251st and beyond by market size on Indian exchanges. They're smaller, less researched, often less liquid — which is exactly why they can compound faster than large, well-known companies over time, and exactly why they can also fall much harder in a downturn or liquidity crunch.

The extra return exists, on average, over long periods. But it isn't a smooth extra 3-4% a year — it comes in lumpy bursts, usually right after the periods that test your patience the most.

Why most people give up at exactly the wrong time

Smallcap crashes don't announce themselves politely. They arrive with news about "overheated valuations" and "regulatory concerns," right when your SIP statement shows red for the first time in years. This is precisely when SIP flows into smallcap funds tend to slow down — and precisely the wrong moment to stop, because it's often close to when the recovery begins. The fund doesn't fail you; the timing of your fear does.

Three checks before you start (or continue) a smallcap SIP

  • Horizon check: Is this money you genuinely won't need for 8-10 years? Smallcaps need time to work through full market cycles.
  • Sizing check: Is smallcap exposure a slice of your equity portfolio (commonly 10-20%), sitting on top of a largecap/flexicap core — not the whole portfolio?
  • Behaviour check: Have you actually lived through a 30%+ fall in any investment before? If not, assume you'll feel it more than you expect, and size accordingly.

A simple way to hold the volatility without flinching

Automate the SIP so the decision to invest each month isn't a decision at all — it's a standing instruction that continues whether the news is loud or quiet. Review the underlying category allocation once a year, not once a week. And keep a written note to yourself, made when markets are calm, reminding you why you started. You'll need it during the next fall, because that's exactly when logic gets hardest to access.

If you're building a broader equity plan, our articles on FD vs mutual fund and on choosing between fund categories can help you decide how much of your portfolio should even be this aggressive in the first place.

The honest verdict

A smallcap SIP is worth the volatility for an investor with time, a defined allocation, and a plan written in advance. It is not worth it for someone who will check the app every day, panic at a 35% drawdown, and exit at the bottom — no return, however large on paper, survives that behaviour. The fund category isn't the risk. The mismatch between your temperament and your allocation is.
