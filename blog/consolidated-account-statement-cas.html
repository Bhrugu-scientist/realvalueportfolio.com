What Is a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS)?

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · August 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: A CAS is a single monthly statement — sent by CAMS or KFintech — that lists every mutual fund you own across every AMC and folio, linked by your PAN. It's your one-stop way to check whether your money is actually where it's supposed to be.

If you invest in mutual funds from more than one AMC — which most people eventually do — you don't get one messy pile of statements to reconcile. India's mutual fund registrars solved this years ago with the CAS. Yet most investors delete the email without opening it. That's a mistake, and here's why.

Who sends it, and why two names?

Almost all AMCs outsource record-keeping to one of two Registrar and Transfer Agents (RTAs): CAMS (Computer Age Management Services) or KFintech (formerly Karvy). If all your funds happen to sit with AMCs served by the same RTA, you'll get one CAS from that RTA covering everything. If your funds are split across both RTAs, CAMS and KFintech now generate a combined CAS between them, so you should still receive one unified PDF — not two separate ones — covering all your mutual fund holdings by PAN.

If you hold any mutual fund units in demat form (bought via a stockbroker), you may also get the broader NSDL/CDSL version, which folds in your equities and bonds too. Most retail MF-only investors will only ever see the CAMS/KFintech version.

What's actually inside a CAS

  • Every folio, every AMC — scheme names, folio numbers, and the AMC each one belongs to
  • Transaction history for the period — purchases, SIP instalments, redemptions, switches, dividend payouts, STP/SWIP entries
  • Units and NAV — units held and the NAV used for every transaction
  • Current value — market value of each holding as on the statement date
  • Nominee and KYC status — often flagged at the bottom, worth glancing at

When do you get one, and what's the password?

A monthly CAS is emailed automatically to your registered email ID only if there was at least one transaction in that month across any of your folios. If a month is completely inactive, you may instead get an annual CAS covering April–March once a year, showing opening and closing balances even with zero activity.

The PDF is password-protected. The password is usually your PAN (in a specific case format) or a PAN + date-of-birth combination — the exact format is mentioned in the email itself. If you've misplaced it, you can request a fresh one directly from the CAMS or KFintech websites using your PAN and registered email.

Why you should actually open it

  1. Spot wrong or duplicate folios — sometimes a distributor change or online purchase creates a second folio instead of adding to an existing one
  2. Check nominee details are filled — SEBI rules now require this; an unchecked CAS can hide a missing nominee for years
  3. Catch unauthorised transactions early — a redemption or switch you didn't request should jump out immediately
  4. Verify your SIPs are actually running — a bounced SIP won't always trigger a loud alert; the CAS shows the gap
  5. See your true total portfolio value — one number instead of five app logins

This ties into a habit worth building alongside checking your CAS: reviewing your expense ratio and direct-vs-regular choices, since the CAS will show you exactly which plan type each folio sits in.

CAS vs your AMC app or advisor portal

Use the app for convenience. Use the CAS as the official, RTA-verified source of truth — especially useful when filing capital gains for tax, applying for a loan against mutual funds, or during estate/succession matters.

A simple monthly habit

Set a reminder for the day your CAS usually lands. Open it, scan the folio list against what you expect to own, check the total value, and glance at the nominee field. Five minutes, once a month. It's the same discipline that matters when you're deciding whether to split money between FDs and mutual funds — you can't manage what you don't actually look at.
