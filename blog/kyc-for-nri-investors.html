KYC for NRI Investors: The Complete 2026 Checklist

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: NRI KYC needs a passport copy, overseas address proof, PAN, a photo, and a FATCA/CRS declaration — plus in-person verification, usually done by video call. Most of it can be completed without visiting India, but mismatched names and outdated address proofs are what cause the delays.

If you've moved abroad and already have a resident KYC on file, you can't simply start investing again on the old record. Status change, fresh documents, and sometimes a new KYC altogether are needed. Here's exactly what to prepare, in what order.

Who counts as an NRI for KYC purposes

For mutual fund KYC, your residential status under FEMA (not just the Income Tax Act) is what matters. If you live outside India for work, business, or any purpose indicating an intention to stay abroad, you're treated as an NRI investor and must use NRI-specific KYC and bank routes — not your old resident folio.

Documents checklist

NRE vs NRO: pick the right bank route first

KYC and your bank account type are linked. You'll generally need an NRE or NRO account before or during the KYC process:

  • NRE account — for foreign earnings; fully repatriable; investments made from here are freely repatriable too
  • NRO account — for India-sourced income (rent, dividends); repatriation is capped and needs extra paperwork

Decide this before KYC, because the AMC will map your folio to this account for redemptions and dividends — changing it later is an extra round of paperwork.

FATCA and CRS — don't skip this

Every NRI investor must submit a FATCA/CRS self-certification declaring their country of tax residency. This isn't optional paperwork — AMCs are legally required to report certain accounts to tax authorities in the relevant country. Leaving this incomplete is one of the most common reasons transactions get frozen after the first investment, not before.

Country-specific restrictions matter more than KYC

Completing KYC doesn't guarantee every fund will accept you. Investors resident in the US and Canada face additional compliance burden (FATCA-related reporting costs) that many AMCs choose not to take on, so they restrict or close NRI subscriptions from these countries for certain schemes. This is an AMC-level investment restriction, separate from your KYC status — check scheme-specific NRI eligibility before assuming you can invest in any fund.

Where it usually goes wrong

  • Name mismatch — passport says one spelling, PAN says another; fix PAN first
  • Stale address proof — documents older than the validity window get rejected
  • Non-English documents — need notarised/certified English translation
  • Old resident KYC not updated — investing through a stale domestic KYC record instead of updating status
  • Missing FATCA form — transactions get restricted even after the folio is opened

The practical sequence

  1. Get/verify your PAN — correct spelling matching your passport
  2. Open an NRE or NRO account based on the source of funds
  3. Gather passport, overseas address proof, and photo
  4. Complete KYC through a KRA or your distributor, including video IPV
  5. Submit FATCA/CRS declaration
  6. Check scheme-level NRI eligibility before placing your first investment

The honest verdict

NRI KYC isn't complicated — it's just unforgiving of small mismatches. One wrong spelling or one expired utility bill can stall an investment for weeks while you're in a different time zone. Get the documents exactly right the first time, and the rest of the process is quick and almost entirely digital.
