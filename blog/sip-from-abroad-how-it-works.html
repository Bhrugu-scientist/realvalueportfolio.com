SIP From Abroad — How It Works

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: NRIs can run a regular SIP in Indian mutual funds using an NRE or NRO account, after completing KYC with a foreign address and a FATCA declaration. The account type you pick decides how easily your money comes back out later — so choose it based on where the money is actually coming from, not convenience.

Many NRIs assume investing from abroad needs a local Indian presence or a complicated workaround. It doesn't. The process is standard — but a few early choices (account type, KYC mode, bank mandate) determine whether your SIP runs smoothly for years or gets stuck in paperwork at redemption time.

Step 1: Open the right bank account

AccountSource of fundsRepatriation
NRE (Non-Resident External)Foreign earnings, converted to INRFully repatriable — principal and gains
NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary)India-sourced income — rent, dividends, pensionCapped at USD 1 million/year, needs CA certificate
FCNRForeign currency depositsUsed for deposits, not directly for MF SIPs

Most NRIs investing fresh foreign income for long-term goals use an NRE account — it keeps the exit simple later. If your SIP is funded from Indian rental income, NRO is the correct, honest choice even though repatriation is slightly more paperwork.

Step 2: Complete NRI KYC

  • PAN card (mandatory)
  • Passport copy and overseas address proof
  • Indian address proof, if you still hold one
  • Overseas bank account proof (NRE/NRO statement or cancelled cheque)
  • Passport-size photo and in-person verification (via video KYC or at an Indian embassy/consulate in some cases)

This is a one-time process per investor, not per fund house, if done through a centralised KYC registry — but documentation standards differ slightly across AMCs, so get it right the first time.

Step 3: FATCA and country restrictions

Every NRI investor must submit a FATCA/CRS self-declaration. For US and Canada-based NRIs, many Indian AMCs restrict or decline new investments because the compliance cost of reporting to US tax authorities is high. A handful of AMCs do accept US/Canada NRIs — check current acceptance before assuming you're excluded. For NRIs in the Gulf, UK, Singapore, and most other countries, there's no such restriction.

Step 4: Set up the SIP mandate

Once KYC is done, the SIP itself works exactly like it does for resident Indians — you choose the amount, date, and duration, and give a standing instruction (e-mandate or NACH) on your NRE/NRO account. The money debits automatically every month, same as any domestic investor. If your account bank doesn't support NACH mandates for NRI accounts, a few AMCs allow manual transfers against each instalment — slower, but workable.

Taxation — what changes for NRIs

Capital gains tax rates on equity and debt mutual funds are broadly the same for NRIs as for residents. The real difference is TDS at source: AMCs deduct tax before paying out redemption proceeds to NRIs, unlike for residents where no TDS applies on equity fund redemptions. If your country has a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) with India, you may be able to claim a lower TDS rate or credit this against your tax liability there — this needs a Tax Residency Certificate and some coordination with a CA.

Common mistakes that cause delays

  • Using a resident Indian bank account instead of NRE/NRO after status changes — this is a compliance issue, not just a technicality
  • Mismatched address proof between KYC and bank records
  • Forgetting to update KYC status from resident to NRI when moving abroad — this alone blocks future transactions
  • Assuming NRE funds can be freely moved in and out of NRO without proper transfer documentation

The honest takeaway

SIP from abroad isn't harder than SIP from India — it just has more boxes to tick upfront. Get the account type, KYC, and FATCA declaration right once, and the monthly mechanics run exactly like they do for any resident investor.
