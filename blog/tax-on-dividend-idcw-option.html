Tax on Dividend / IDCW Option in Mutual Funds (2026)

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, formerly called "dividend") payouts are added to your total income and taxed at your slab rate — not at the lower capital gains rate. For most investors, this makes the growth option more tax-efficient.

"Dividend option" mutual funds still get chosen by people who like the idea of regular payouts. But since a 2020 tax law change, the way this is taxed makes it one of the least efficient ways to receive money from a mutual fund — especially if you're in a higher tax bracket. Here's exactly how it works.

What IDCW actually is

IDCW stands for Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal — SEBI renamed "dividend option" in 2021 to make this clearer. When a fund pays IDCW, it isn't handing you extra profit. It sells a small part of the fund's assets and pays you out of your own NAV. If the NAV was Rs 50 and the fund pays Rs 2 IDCW, the NAV drops to Rs 48. You haven't gained anything — you've just converted part of your own investment into cash, and that cash is now taxable.

How IDCW is taxed today

AspectRule
TaxabilityAdded to your total income, taxed at your income tax slab rate
Who pays the taxYou, the unit holder — not the fund
TDS by fund house10% TDS if IDCW from one fund house exceeds Rs 5,000 in a financial year
TDS for NRIs20% (or DTAA rate, if lower and documentation is furnished)
ReportingShown under "Income from Other Sources" in your ITR

This is very different from the pre-2020 regime, where the fund itself paid a Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) and the payout reached you tax-free in your hands. That system is gone. Now the tax liability sits fully with you.

IDCW vs growth — the real comparison

IDCW optionGrowth option
When tax is triggeredEvery time a payout is madeOnly on redemption
Tax rate appliedYour income slab rateLTCG (12.5% above Rs 1.25L/yr for equity) or STCG (20%)
CompoundingInterrupted — payout leaves the fundUninterrupted — full amount stays invested
Control over timingFund decides when to payYou decide when to redeem

For someone in the 30% tax bracket, IDCW income is taxed at 30%, while an equity fund held over a year and redeemed under growth option is taxed at just 12.5% LTCG (above the annual exemption). That gap alone can be worth lakhs over a long investment horizon.

Why people still pick IDCW

  • They want a regular "income-like" cash flow, similar to an FD interest credit
  • Retirees sometimes use IDCW from debt or hybrid funds to fund monthly expenses
  • Some feel more comfortable "locking in" gains periodically

These are legitimate needs — but a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) on the growth option usually achieves the same cash flow far more tax-efficiently, because each SWP withdrawal is taxed only on the gain portion at capital gains rates, not on the entire amount at your slab rate.

A quick example

Suppose you invest Rs 10 lakh and it grows to Rs 13 lakh over 3 years.

  • IDCW route: Fund pays out Rs 3 lakh across the period as IDCW. All Rs 3 lakh is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate — say 30% = Rs 90,000 tax.
  • Growth + redeem route: You redeem the full Rs 13 lakh. Gain of Rs 3 lakh is LTCG. After the Rs 1.25 lakh annual exemption, roughly Rs 1.75 lakh is taxed at 12.5% = about Rs 21,875 tax (single-year redemption assumption).

The exact numbers vary with holding period and exemption use, but the direction is consistent: growth option is almost always more tax-efficient than IDCW for wealth accumulation goals.

What to actually check in your portfolio

  • Open your account statement and check if any fund is marked "IDCW" — many investors inherit this by default from old folios
  • Check your Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the income tax portal — IDCW income is now visible there and pre-filled in your ITR
  • If you don't need periodic cash flow, consider switching to the growth option (note: switching itself may trigger capital gains tax, so plan around your holding period)
  • If you need cash flow, compare an SWP on growth option against your current IDCW payout math

This ties closely into how you think about FD vs mutual fund choices — in both cases, the instrument that "feels" like regular income isn't always the one that builds the most wealth after tax.

The honest verdict

IDCW isn't a scam and it isn't useless — for someone who genuinely needs periodic cash and is in a lower tax bracket, it can still work. But for most investors building long-term wealth, the growth option combined with a planned SWP (if cash flow is needed later) is the more tax-efficient, more compounding-friendly choice. Don't pick the option because of the name — pick it because you ran the numbers.
