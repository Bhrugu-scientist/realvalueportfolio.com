Debt Fund vs FD: Which Is Better for Safe Money in 2026?

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: After the 2023 tax rule change, debt funds and FDs are much closer than people assume. Debt funds still win on liquidity and flexibility; FDs win on simplicity and a guaranteed number. Neither is risk-free — they just carry different kinds of risk.

Both debt mutual funds and fixed deposits get filed under "safe investments" in most people's heads. That's only half true. They behave very differently once you look closely at taxation, liquidity, and what can actually go wrong. Here's the comparison without the sales pitch.

Head to head

The tax rule that changed everything

Before April 2023, debt funds held over 3 years got indexation benefit — a genuine edge over FDs for long-term money. That benefit is gone for most debt funds now. Today, both FD interest and debt fund gains are taxed at your income slab rate. This single change removed the biggest reason many investors chose debt funds over FDs for long holding periods. It didn't make debt funds bad — it just made the comparison fairer.

What FDs hide well

  • Interest is taxed every year even if you don't withdraw it — a cash-flow drag for high-tax-bracket investors
  • Breaking an FD early costs you a penalty and resets your negotiating position on rate
  • Reinvestment risk — when your FD matures, you may have to reinvest at a lower rate if rates have fallen

What debt funds hide well

  • NAV can fall on days when interest rates rise sharply — a low-duration fund will barely notice, a long-duration fund can dip meaningfully
  • Credit risk — a fund holding lower-rated bonds can suffer if one issuer defaults; this has happened in India before
  • Expense ratio quietly reduces your return every year, something an FD doesn't have

Where each one genuinely fits

SituationBetter fit
Emergency fund, need instant accessLiquid/overnight debt fund or savings-linked FD
Fixed goal date, don't want any surpriseFD matched to that date
Parking money for 6–18 months, want flexibilityShort-duration debt fund
You're in the 30% tax bracket and want simplicityEither — the tax gap has narrowed, so pick based on liquidity need

Checks to run before choosing a debt fund

  • What is the fund's average maturity or duration? Shorter duration = less interest-rate sensitivity
  • What is the credit quality of the portfolio — mostly AAA/sovereign, or does it dip into lower-rated paper for extra yield?
  • What is the expense ratio, and how does the fund's actual net return compare to a comparable FD over 3 years?
  • Is there an exit load, and for how long?

The honest verdict

This isn't a fight where one side wins outright. FDs give you a number you can write down today and rely on. Debt funds give you liquidity and flexibility, with a small amount of variability in return. For most people, a mix works best — an FD or two for goals with a fixed date, and a debt fund for money that needs to stay flexible.
