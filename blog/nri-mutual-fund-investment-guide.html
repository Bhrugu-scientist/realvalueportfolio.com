NRI Mutual Fund Investment — Full Guide (2026)

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer: Yes, NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds through an NRE or NRO account after completing KYC and FATCA declaration. The process is simple once set up — but choosing the wrong account type, skipping FATCA, or ignoring TDS can cost you money and paperwork headaches later.

If you're an NRI with family roots in India — and plenty of Bharuch families have members across the US, UK, Gulf and Canada — Indian equity markets are often a better long-term opportunity than where you currently live. But the mechanics of investing from abroad trip up even financially savvy people. Here's the full picture.

Step 1: Get your account type right

Source of fundsForeign income remitted to IndiaIncome earned in India (rent, dividends, pension)

Your mutual fund folio gets tagged as NRE or NRO based on the bank account used for investment — this tag determines how redemption proceeds are repatriated later. Pick it carefully at the start; switching later is possible but adds paperwork.

Step 2: KYC and FATCA — non-negotiable

Before any mutual fund investment, NRIs must complete:

  • KYC (Know Your Customer) — passport copy, overseas address proof, PAN card, and in-person or video verification
  • FATCA/CRS self-declaration — mandatory for every investor, but especially scrutinised for US and Canada tax residents

Without a valid, up-to-date KYC and FATCA on file, your purchase can get rejected or your folio frozen for further transactions — a common and avoidable source of frustration.

The US/Canada complication

This is the part most guides skip. Many Indian AMCs restrict or outright decline fresh investments from NRIs resident in the US and Canada, because FATCA compliance and reporting obligations are heavier for those jurisdictions. It's not a blanket ban — a number of fund houses do accept US/Canada NRIs, often with additional documentation — but your choices are narrower than for an NRI in the UAE, UK, or Singapore. Check with the specific fund house (not just your distributor) before assuming you can invest in a particular scheme.

Step 3: How to actually invest

  • Through a distributor/advisor in India — most practical for NRIs; paperwork, KYC coordination and ongoing service handled for you
  • Directly via AMC websites — possible but each AMC has its own NRI process and forms
  • Power of Attorney (PoA) — some NRIs give a trusted family member in India a PoA to handle transactions locally, useful but needs careful drafting

Taxation and TDS — the part that surprises people

Capital gains tax rates for NRIs are the same as for resident Indians — equity funds get preferential long-term rates, debt funds are taxed at slab rate. The difference is TDS (tax deducted at source): AMCs are required to deduct tax on NRI redemptions upfront, often at a rate higher than your actual final liability. You then claim the excess back when filing your Indian income tax return. Keep every redemption statement and TDS certificate (Form 16A) — you'll need them for the refund claim.

Also check the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) between India and your country of residence — it can reduce your overall tax burden if claimed correctly, usually requiring a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your resident country.

Repatriation — getting your money back out

For NRE-linked investments, repatriation of both capital and gains is generally straightforward. For NRO-linked investments, repatriation is capped (currently USD 1 million per financial year, subject to conditions) and requires a chartered accountant's certificate (Form 15CB) plus a declaration (Form 15CA) before the bank will remit funds abroad.

Practical checklist before you start

  • Decide NRE vs NRO based on the source of the money, not convenience
  • Complete KYC with overseas address proof and PAN
  • File FATCA/CRS declaration — check AMC-specific restrictions if you're US/Canada based
  • Use a nominee and keep your mailing address and mobile/email updated for all folios
  • Track TDS certificates for your annual tax return
  • Understand DTAA benefits with a cross-border tax advisor if your situation is complex

If you're also wondering whether equity exposure makes sense given your goals and timeline, see our guide on FD vs mutual fund for the long-term return argument, and our piece on SIP vs lumpsum investing if you're deciding how to deploy a lump sum of overseas savings.

The honest verdict

NRI investing in Indian mutual funds is very doable and, for many NRIs, a smart way to stay connected to India's growth story while earning abroad. The friction is entirely in the paperwork — account type, KYC, FATCA, TDS, repatriation rules — not in the investment decision itself. Get the structure right once, and the actual investing becomes as simple as it is for any resident Indian.
