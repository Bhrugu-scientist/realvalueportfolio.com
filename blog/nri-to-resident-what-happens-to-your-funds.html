NRI to Resident — What Happens to Your Mutual Funds

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: Your mutual fund units don't disappear or get frozen when you move back to India — but your status, bank mandate and tax treatment all need to be formally updated. Skipping this causes wrong TDS, mismatched bank credits, and compliance headaches later. It's a paperwork job, not a crisis, but it has to be done properly.

Thousands of NRIs return to India every year — for family, for retirement, for a new chapter. Somewhere between the shipping container and the new SIM card, the mutual fund folios you built over years abroad quietly get forgotten. Here's exactly what changes, and the order to fix it in.

Why your status even matters to a mutual fund

Mutual funds aren't just tracking your money — they're tracking your regulatory status under FEMA (for investment route) and under the Income Tax Act (for taxation). Your residential status for tax purposes is determined by your physical stay in India in a financial year — broadly, 182 days or more makes you a resident, independent of your citizenship or visa. The moment that status flips, the fund house is legally required to treat your folio differently.

Step-by-step: what to actually do

  1. Update your KYC record first. This is the master record linked across all your folios (via PAN) through KRA agencies. Submit a fresh KYC form marking your status as "Resident Individual," with updated Indian address proof.
  2. Convert your bank accounts. Under FEMA, NRE and NRO accounts must be converted to resident accounts (or an RFC account, if you want to retain foreign currency flexibility) once you become a resident. Don't let this linger — mutual fund redemption credits should match your current bank mandate.
  3. Submit a folio-level status change request with each AMC or your RTA (CAMS/KFintech), updating bank details, tax status and FATCA declaration together — not separately — to avoid mismatched records.
  4. Check your TDS trail. As an NRI, TDS was likely deducted on every redemption's capital gain. Once you're resident, this should stop. If TDS continues due to a records lag, you can claim it back while filing your return — but it's cleaner to fix the status promptly.
  5. Review your nominee and joint holding details — these sometimes carry old overseas address data that should be refreshed along with your own.

What does NOT change

  • Your units, folio number, and investment history stay exactly as they are — nothing is redeemed or reset.
  • Past capital gains already taxed (via TDS as an NRI) are not revisited — only future transactions follow resident rules.
  • You don't need to exit and re-enter funds to "convert" your investment — only the paperwork around it changes.

The repatriation question

If you plan to eventually move abroad again, or want to repatriate accumulated funds, keep your RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account option in mind — it lets returning NRIs hold foreign currency earned abroad with more repatriation flexibility than a plain resident account. This is worth discussing with your bank before you convert everything, especially if a chunk of your mutual fund corpus originated from foreign income.

A common mistake

Many returning NRIs keep redeeming into an old NRO account out of habit, long after becoming resident — which creates a mismatch between your declared tax status and your actual banking trail. If you're also reviewing your overall portfolio exposure after the move, it helps to look at this alongside a broader FD vs mutual fund comparison for how to park the funds you've repatriated, since priorities around liquidity and safety often shift once you're back and settling in.

The honest verdict

Your mutual fund investments survive the move home just fine — mechanically, nothing breaks. What breaks is accuracy: wrong tax deducted, redemption credited to a dead account, mismatched KYC flagged years later during a routine audit. None of this is dramatic, but all of it is avoidable with one clean round of paperwork done within a few months of returning. Do it once, do it fully, and your folios quietly go back to working for you as a resident investor.
