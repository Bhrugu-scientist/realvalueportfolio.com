NRI Repatriation of Mutual Fund Proceeds: How the Money Actually Comes Back

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 7 min read
Short answer: Whether your redemption money can be freely sent abroad depends on which account funded the original investment — NRE-linked money repatriates freely, NRO-linked money is capped at USD 1 million/year and needs a CA certificate (Form 15CB) plus Form 15CA before the bank will remit it.

Most NRIs discover the repatriation rules only when they try to redeem — and that's the worst time to be surprised. The mutual fund transaction is the easy part. Getting the proceeds out of India is where the paperwork lives. Here's the full path, step by step.

Step 1: It starts with how you invested, not how you redeem

Repatriation rules are decided at the investment stage, not the redemption stage. There are two routes NRIs use:

  • NRE (repatriable) route — You invested using money from your NRE account or a direct foreign inward remittance. Both principal and gains are fully, freely repatriable.
  • NRO (non-repatriable) route — You invested using income earned in India (rent, dividends, salary credited to NRO) or simply used your NRO account. Repatriation is capped and requires tax compliance paperwork.

This is why your folio's bank mandate (NRE vs NRO) at the time of purchase matters more than people realise.

NRE vs NRO: the practical difference

Source of fundsForeign earnings / NRE accountIndian-sourced income (rent, local salary, etc.)

Step 2: TDS is deducted automatically at redemption

Unlike resident investors, mutual funds deduct TDS on capital gains for NRIs at the time of redemption itself — you don't wait till tax filing. Broadly:

  • Equity funds: STCG (under 1 year) and LTCG (over 1 year, above the exemption threshold) are taxed, with TDS deducted before payout.
  • Debt funds: gains are taxed at your applicable slab rate under current rules, with TDS deducted accordingly.

The net amount (after TDS) is what credits to your NRE or NRO bank account linked to the folio. You can later claim a refund or adjust via your income tax return if actual tax liability under DTAA is lower.

Step 3: Moving money from NRO to abroad — the 15CA/15CB process

If your proceeds land in an NRO account, repatriating them abroad (beyond routine small remittances) typically needs:

  • Form 15CB — a certificate from a practicing Chartered Accountant confirming the nature of the remittance and that applicable tax has been paid or will be paid.
  • Form 15CA — a self-declaration filed online on the income tax e-filing portal, based on the CA's 15CB.
  • Your bank's own remittance request form (A2 form) and supporting KYC documents.

Banks will not process the outward remittance without these, so budget a few extra days versus a pure NRE-route redemption where the money often moves almost as fast as a resident redemption.

Step 4: Check DTAA before you assume the TDS rate is final

If you're tax resident in a country that has a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) with India, you may be eligible for a lower withholding rate than the standard TDS applied by the fund house. This requires submitting a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F to the AMC or RTA, ideally before redemption so the correct rate is applied upfront — reclaiming excess TDS later through an Indian tax return is possible but slower.

Common mistakes NRIs make

  • Mixing NRE and NRO funds in the same folio, making the repatriation route ambiguous
  • Redeeming without updating bank mandate — proceeds bounce back if the linked account is closed or dormant
  • Not engaging a CA in advance for 15CB, causing redemption-to-remittance delays of weeks
  • Ignoring DTAA benefits and overpaying TDS that then needs a tax return to recover

The honest takeaway

Repatriation isn't blocked for NRIs — it's gated by documentation. Keep your NRE and NRO investments cleanly separated, engage a CA early for the 15CA/15CB cycle, and check your DTAA eligibility before redeeming large amounts. Do that, and the money moves almost as smoothly as it would for a resident investor.
