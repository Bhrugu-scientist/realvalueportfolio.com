How Much Wealth Needs a Dedicated Advisor?

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · October 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: There's no magic crore. A Rs 5 lakh portfolio with three confusing goals and a panic-prone investor needs an advisor more than a Rs 50 lakh portfolio sitting calmly in two index funds. The real triggers are complexity, time, and behaviour — not a number on a net worth statement.

"Should I wait until I have more money before getting an advisor?" is one of the most common questions we hear in Bharuch. It assumes advice is a luxury reserved for the wealthy. In reality, the earlier mistakes happen, the more they compound — so smaller portfolios sometimes need guidance more urgently.

The wrong way to think about it

Many investors use an arbitrary threshold — "I'll see an advisor once I cross Rs 25 lakh" — as a way to delay a decision they're unsure about. But a threshold based purely on portfolio size misses the point. A techie with a Rs 8 lakh portfolio spread across 11 random mutual funds, two insurance-cum-investment policies, and no idea what his asset allocation is has a bigger problem than someone with Rs 40 lakh neatly split between two funds and a clear goal.

The real triggers that matter

SituationDIY usually worksAdvisor adds real value
Number of goalsOne clear goal (e.g. retirement)Multiple overlapping goals (house, kids' education, retirement)
Income sourcesSingle salary, simple SIPsBusiness income, RSUs, rental, inheritance, multiple accounts
Time availableYou enjoy reviewing funds quarterlyNo time or interest to track anything
Past behaviourStayed invested through 2020 and 2022 dipsPanic-sold or paused SIPs during a crash before
KnowledgeUnderstand expense ratio, asset allocation, taxationConfused by category names, chasing star ratings

Complexity compounds faster than money

A single salaried person with one SIP and one goal can genuinely manage well alone for years. But add a second income, a property purchase, ageing parents needing support, a child's overseas education goal, and a business that pays irregularly — and suddenly the portfolio needs coordination, not just contributions. This is when complexity, not wealth, crosses the threshold.

The behaviour test nobody talks about

Here's an uncomfortable truth: most underperformance in Indian mutual fund investing doesn't come from picking the wrong fund. It comes from investors buying high (when markets feel good) and selling low (when markets crash and feel terrible). Data from fund houses repeatedly shows investor returns lagging fund returns — the gap is almost entirely behavioural.

Ask yourself honestly: did you pause or reduce your SIP in March 2020? Did you check your portfolio daily during a correction and feel the urge to exit? If yes, an advisor's biggest value to you isn't fund selection — it's being the voice that stops you from making an expensive emotional decision. That value exists whether your portfolio is Rs 3 lakh or Rs 3 crore.

What a dedicated advisor actually does

  • Builds a written plan mapped to your specific goals and timelines, not generic templates
  • Reviews asset allocation periodically and rebalances when it drifts
  • Explains the "why" before every change, so you understand your own portfolio
  • Acts as a behavioural check during volatile markets — the single highest-value service
  • Coordinates investments with tax, insurance, and liquidity needs holistically

Signs you've outgrown DIY

  • You own 8+ mutual funds and couldn't explain why for half of them
  • You've never calculated your actual asset allocation (equity : debt : gold ratio)
  • You bought a fund because a relative or app notification suggested it
  • Your goals have multiplied but your portfolio structure hasn't changed in years
  • You've felt genuine panic during a market fall and acted on it

The honest verdict

Don't wait for a number. Wait for honesty about your own situation — your time, your knowledge, and your temperament under stress. A small, simple, well-behaved portfolio can thrive without an advisor. A small, messy, emotionally-driven one often can't grow at all without one. Wealth size is almost irrelevant; self-awareness about complexity and behaviour is everything.
