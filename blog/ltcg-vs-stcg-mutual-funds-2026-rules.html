LTCG vs STCG on Mutual Funds — 2026 Rules

By Bhrugu Thakkar · Real Value (ARN 24454) · September 2026 · 6 min read
Short answer: Hold equity mutual funds for more than 1 year and your gains are taxed at 12.5% LTCG above a ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption. Sell within a year and it's a flat 20% STCG. Debt funds get no LTCG benefit at all — gains are always taxed at your income slab, no matter how long you hold.

Tax on mutual funds isn't complicated once you separate two things: what you're invested in (equity or debt), and how long you held it. Get these two answered, and the rate is fixed. Here's the full 2026 picture.

Holding period thresholds

Fund TypeShort-term (STCG)Long-term (LTCG)
Equity fund (≥65% equity)Held ≤ 1 yearHeld > 1 year
Debt fund (<35% equity)No LTCG distinctionAll gains taxed as per slab
Hybrid (35–65% equity)Held ≤ 2 yearsHeld > 2 years

Tax rates that apply

Equity FundsDebt Funds
Short-term gainFlat 20%Added to income, taxed at slab
Long-term gain12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh/year exemptionNot applicable — always slab rate
Indexation benefitNoNo

Notice the exemption line: the first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term equity gains in a financial year is tax-free. That's per person, per year — and it resets every April. This is the single most under-used piece of tax planning in Indian retail investing.

The ₹1.25 lakh exemption — use it every year

Because this exemption resets annually, letting several years of gains pile up and then redeeming everything at once wastes it. A simple habit: each financial year, review your equity mutual fund gains and consider harvesting up to ₹1.25 lakh of profit (sell and, if you still want the position, reinvest). Done correctly, that portion of your gain is permanently tax-free, year after year, instead of being taxed later in one lump sum.

Worked example

Suppose you have ₹3 lakh of long-term equity gains this year:

  • First ₹1.25 lakh — exempt, ₹0 tax
  • Remaining ₹1.75 lakh — taxed at 12.5% = ₹21,875

Compare that to selling short-term (within 1 year): the entire ₹3 lakh gets taxed at 20% = ₹60,000. The holding period alone is the difference between ₹21,875 and ₹60,000 in tax on the exact same profit. This is why patience is a tax strategy, not just an investing virtue.

Debt funds — the rule that changed

Since the 2023 rule change (still applicable in 2026), debt mutual funds get no LTCG benefit at all. Whether you hold a debt fund for 6 months or 6 years, the entire gain is added to your taxable income and taxed at your slab rate. This makes debt funds less tax-efficient than they used to be — though they can still make sense for short-term parking, diversification, or liquidity, compared against options like FDs (see our FD vs mutual fund comparison).

Practical checks before you redeem

  • Check the purchase date of each lot — SIP units each carry their own holding-period clock (FIFO — first bought, first sold)
  • Check your fund's equity allocation if it's a hybrid or dynamic fund — this decides which tax table applies
  • Track your cumulative LTCG gains for the financial year so you don't accidentally cross ₹1.25 lakh without a plan
  • Don't let tax alone drive investment decisions — a bad fund held longer for tax reasons is still a bad fund

The honest takeaway

The tax code is quietly rewarding patience and penalizing frequent trading. An equity fund held over a year, redeemed within the annual exemption in stages, is taxed lightly. The same money churned in and out within months is taxed hard. If you're building long-term wealth anyway, the tax rules mostly work in your favour — you just need to know where the lines are.
